________________________________________
1. Team Purpose
Our team is responsible for designing and delivering a working Support Deflection MVP chatbot for Northstar Retail Co.
The chatbot will demonstrate how repetitive customer-support tickets can be resolved without manual intervention by providing customers with immediate answers to:
•	Order-status questions
•	Return and refund questions
The objective is not to build a production-ready system, but to deliver a functional, demoable MVP that proves the support-deflection approach can work.
________________________________________
2. Sprint Objectives
By the final deadline, the team will deliver:
1.	A working chatbot prototype covering at least two ticket categories.

3.	A 1-page go-live readiness note.

4. Rules of Communication
4.1 Primary Communication Channel
The team's agreed communication channel will be:
Primary: WhatsApp/Teams group
Project Work: Shared project workspace/repository
Urgent Issues: Team group + direct mention of the responsible member
4.2 Response Time
During active sprint hours:
•	Team members should acknowledge important messages within 30 minutes.
•	Questions blocking another member's work should be answered as quickly as reasonably possible.
•	If a member will be unavailable for more than 1 hour, they must inform the team.
4.3 Communication Standards
All members agree to:
•	Communicate respectfully.
•	Keep project discussions focused.
•	Avoid personal attacks.
•	Raise problems early instead of hiding them.
•	Ask for clarification when requirements are unclear.
•	Clearly communicate when a task is completed.
•	Avoid making major project decisions without informing the team.
•	Use evidence and project requirements when disagreeing.
4.4 Meeting Rule
Every team meeting must have:
•	A clear objective.
•	A designated person responsible for notes/actions.
•	Clearly assigned next steps.
•	A short status update from each member where necessary.
________________________________________
5. Work & Deadline Rules
Because this is a four-day sprint, deadlines are treated as commitments, not suggestions.
Task Completion Rule
A task is not considered complete merely because someone says:
"I've done it."
It must satisfy its agreed Definition of Done and be visible in the project workspace/repository where applicable.
Deadline Escalation
If a member realizes that a task may miss its deadline, they must notify the Project Lead before the deadline, not after it.
The team will then:
1.	Identify the blocker.
2.	Reassign or split the task if necessary.
3.	Reduce non-essential scope.
4.	Protect the core MVP.
Scope Rule
If time becomes limited, the team will prioritize:
Working chatbot > testing > evidence/audit trail > documentation > visual polish
We will not sacrifice a working MVP merely to add unnecessary features.

7. Definition of "Done" for the Sprint
The project is considered complete only when:
•	The chatbot runs successfully.
•	It handles at least two ticket categories.
•	A customer can complete the intended journey without human assistance.
•	Appropriate responses are provided.
•	Unknown/unsupported questions receive an appropriate fallback response.
•	The main flows have been tested.
•	Critical bugs have been resolved.
•	The project contains evidence of genuine collaboration.
•	The go-live readiness note is complete.
•	The team can demonstrate the chatbot end-to-end.

8. Conflict Resolution Procedure
Disagreements are expected in collaborative work. The team agrees to resolve them professionally.
Level 1 — Direct Discussion
The members involved discuss the issue privately or as a small group.
Each person gets an opportunity to explain their position.
The team focuses on:
•	Project requirements
•	Evidence
•	Time constraints
•	Customer value
•	Technical feasibility
Level 2 — Team Discussion
If the disagreement cannot be resolved, the issue is brought to the full team.
The team discusses the available options and attempts to reach consensus.
Level 3 — Project Lead Decision
If consensus cannot be reached and the decision is blocking progress, the Project Lead makes the final project decision after considering:
1.	Assessment requirements.
2.	MVP scope.
3.	Available time.
4.	Technical feasibility.
5.	Risk to the final deliverable.
Level 4 — Escalation
Serious issues involving non-participation, misconduct or unresolved responsibility disputes will be documented and escalated to plp academy.
________________________________________
9. Non-Participation Rule
If a member repeatedly fails to complete assigned work:
1.	The issue will first be raised directly with the member.
2.	The team will determine whether there is a legitimate blocker.
3.	Work may be redistributed to protect the deadline.
4.	The contribution history will remain visible through the project's audit trail.
5.	Persistent non-participation will be documented.
The purpose is not punishment; it is to protect the team's ability to deliver.
________________________________________
10. Quality Rule
Before anything is marked complete, the responsible member must ask:
"Would I be comfortable demonstrating this to the Northstar client?"
If the answer is no, the task is not done.
________________________________________
11. Decision-Making Principles
When choosing between alternatives, the team will prioritize:
Customer value → MVP requirements → Reliability → Time → Simplicity → Visual polish
We will avoid overengineering.
A simple feature that works is preferable to an impressive feature that cannot be demonstrated reliably.
